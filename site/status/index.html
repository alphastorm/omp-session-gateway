Stable v0.6.1

Known limits — part of the claim

  • TUN mode is mandatory. With userspace-networking tailscaled every tailnet peer arrives as a loopback peer, and the gateway fails closed rather than believing an identity header.
  • Never enable Tailscale Funnel. There is no supported public-Internet path.
  • Android radio transitions can wedge Chrome's process-wide network stack while Android stays healthy; the PWA retries and, after 45 s of visible failure, opens force-stop/reopen help.
  • Relay assurance is 30 minutes, not eight hours. The founder explicitly replaced the eight-hour gate with this fresh check, as for v0.4.1 and v0.4.0. Eight-hour endurance was not rerun or claimed; residual prolonged-operation risk is accepted. No bounded-memory-growth claim is made.
  • Specialized attention, branch/resume, and new media qualification are outside this claim; these physical checks do not qualify those separate lanes. Background Web Push is qualified on the Pixel only, and its force-stop and Doze outcomes are observed variants, never guaranteed delivery.
  • Windows is qualified as Windows Server 2025 x86-64, started at interactive logon, not at boot. Other Windows versions are supported and CI-tested, including a daily canary that drives stock OMP's named pipe through the gateway, but not qualified.
  • In v0.5.0 and earlier, an update can close a page in use: on the first visit after a gateway upgrade, the new service worker's activation can navigate a pending launch or an open View/Control page back to the directory, because Chromium reports each page's creation URL. The first post-release View/Control smoke failures, including v0.4.0's return to the directory after View, match this navigation. The v0.5.1 fix never navigates from the worker; see the release ledger.
  • Untrusted local accounts are out of scope: a direct loopback caller can forge non-cryptographic identity headers on a shared shell host.
  • Portal Tunnel and self-hosted or proxied relays are unsupported; the gateway keeps OMP's existing end-to-end-encrypted relay.

Machine-readable truth: stable release lock · upstream lock · compatibility matrix · release ledger (authoritative where they disagree)

The v0.6.1-prealpha.1 qualification passed every lane on its first attempt in its second campaign. The first campaign on the same candidate failed only background Push: a clear sent just after Airplane recovery arrived 61 s after the answer, and the lane allowed 60 s. The lane now allows the 160-second recovery window after a radio change; that receipt is archived unchanged, and the release ledger records the failure. Final cleanup measured zero gateway processes, zero listeners and zero live OMP hosts on the retained Mac, with the OMP binary and source removed; the Windows VM, its firewall and its tailnet node were destroyed. A clean stable-channel build of the promotion tree and the published stable archive each matched all 46 non-metadata candidate files.

Fork-era release history

v0.3.0 was approved 2026-09-09 against exact patched OMP v18.1.14 observed 2026-09-08, with Bun 1.4.0. Its signed candidate v0.3.0-prealpha.3 was independently qualified and runtime-equivalent. The historical matrix was Debian 13 (trixie) x86-64, macOS 26.6.1 arm64 (Mac14,3), and Chrome 152.0.7977.75 on Android 17 (Pixel 10 Pro). Its rollback predecessor was v0.2.1, with separately activated exact patched OMP v17.4.1.

The source baseline moved to stock mainline OMP v18.1.20 on 2026-09-14 after upstream merged the collaboration registry. Fork-era qualification does not transfer to this architecture.
